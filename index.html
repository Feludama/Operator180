Commander's Intent

One operator, three businesses, one enlistment goal — 180 days to prove you can run the ventures on systems (not adrenaline) and arrive at MEPS ASVAB-ready and AFT-strong. Two outcomes, one disciplined day.

The Mission — read this first, every week

Build the businesses so they run without you, while forging the body and mind to enlist. By Day 180 you make a clean, informed go / no-go on the Army with all three ventures at a documented run-rate and every enlistment gate (ASVAB, AFT, paperwork) either cleared or scheduled. Nothing about this plan forces the decision — it removes every excuse from it.

Rules of engagement: Discipline over motivation. Systems over heroics. Progress logged, not remembered. Miss a day → resume, never restart. This is a living manual — we adjust it at every phase review.

The 5 core sales scripts

Adapt the wording to your voice — keep the structure. These work across all three businesses; swap the offer.

1 · Cold outreach (open the door)

Nebula Digital — DM / email to a local business with a weak site

You: "Hi [Name] — I build websites and AI tools for [industry] businesses here in Houston. I looked at [their site] and spotted 2 quick things costing you leads. Want me to send a 60-second Loom walking through them? No pitch, just useful."

Goal: give value first, earn the reply. The free audit is the hook.

KNF Total Clean — neighborhood / referral outreach

You: "Hi! We're KNF Total Clean, cleaning a few homes on [street/area] already. We have two openings for a recurring spot this month and I'd love to earn yours. Can I text you a quick quote — first clean's [offer]?"

Goal: social proof (already nearby) + scarcity (two openings) + low-risk first clean.

HomeCraft — past-customer reactivation

You: "Hi [Name], it's Ferran with HomeCraft. It's been about a year since we [serviced your HVAC]. Heading into [season] I'm reaching out to past clients first — want me to get you on the schedule for a tune-up before we book up? Takes 5 minutes to set."

Goal: warmest list you own is past customers. Seasonal timing + priority framing.

2 · Discovery call (diagnose before you prescribe)

Universal discovery frame — ask, don't pitch

You: "Before I recommend anything — walk me through what's going on now. What made you reach out today?"

Then dig with: "How are you handling it currently? · What's that costing you (time/money/stress)? · If this were solved, what changes for you? · What's your timeline?"

Close the discovery: "Got it. So the priority is [play back their words]. Here's what I'd do…" — now you prescribe, using their language.

3 · The offer & price (say it, then stop talking)

Present → anchor → silence

You: "For what you described, I'd recommend [package]. That's [price], and it includes [3 outcomes, not features]. We could start [specific date]."

Then say nothing. The first person to speak after the price loses. Let them react.

If two options help: offer a good + better ("Most clients in your spot go with [better] because…") — never more than two.

4 · Objection handling (agree, reframe, ask)

They sayYou say
"It's too expensive.""Totally fair to weigh it. Compared to [cost of the problem continuing], most clients find it pays for itself in [timeframe]. What number were you expecting?"
"I need to think about it.""Makes sense — what specifically do you want to think through? Often I can answer it right now." (Surfaces the real objection.)
"Let me check with my partner/spouse.""Of course. What do you think they'll want to know? Let's get you the answers so it's an easy yes."
"Can you do it cheaper?""I can adjust scope, not quality. We could drop [X] to hit your budget — want me to re-quote that way?" (Protect price; flex scope.)
"I'll get back to you.""Sounds good — I'll hold [date/slot] for you until [day]. Can I check back Thursday?" (Always set the next step.)

5 · Close & follow-up (assume the sale, then chase)

Assumptive close

You: "Great — I'll send the agreement now and we'll start [date]. I just need [deposit/info] to lock it in. Easiest for you: card or transfer?"

Follow-up cadence (money is in the follow-up)

Day 0: recap + agreement. Day 2: "Any questions before we start?" Day 5: value nudge (case study / tip). Day 10: "Closing the loop — want me to hold your spot or circle back next quarter?"

Rule: 80% of sales need 5+ touches. Most people quit at 1. Log every follow-up in your pipeline.

Mind Track · 04

ASVAB Road Map

The ASVAB decides whether you get in and which jobs you can pick. Your leverage is the AFQT (four subtests) for eligibility, then line scores for your target MOS. Study smart: the four AFQT subtests are worth the most — start there.

How scoring works

AFQT — the gate score

Your AFQT is a percentile (1–99) built from just four subtests:

  • AR — Arithmetic Reasoning (math word problems)
  • MK — Mathematics Knowledge (algebra, geometry)
  • WK — Word Knowledge (vocabulary)
  • PC — Paragraph Comprehension (reading)

WK + PC combine into Verbal Expression (VE). AFQT ≈ 2×VE + AR + MK, then converted to a percentile.

Army targets

Minimum to enlistAFQT 31
Waiver floor (case-by-case)~26
Your Phase 3 target50+
Your Phase 4 target60–70+
Bonuses / job flexibility50+

Higher AFQT = more MOS options + enlistment bonuses. Don't aim for "just passing." Aim for 60+ so you choose the job.

Line scores → your job (MOS)

Beyond AFQT, the Army combines subtests into line-score composites. Your target MOS sets which ones matter. Examples:

MOSJobKey line score(s)
11BInfantrymanCO 87
31BMilitary PoliceST 91
68WCombat MedicGT 107 · ST 101
25-seriesSignal / ITEL / GT (high)
17CCyber OperationsGT 110 · ST 112

Composites: GT = Arithmetic Reasoning + Verbal Expression (matters for most good jobs — protect it). ST, EL, GM, MM, CO pull in General Science, Electronics, Mechanical Comprehension, Auto & Shop. Confirm exact numbers with a recruiter — they shift.

Study strategy by subtest

Priority 1 — the AFQT four

  • Math Knowledge: relearn fractions, exponents, algebra, geometry formulas. Most improvable area — pure practice.
  • Arithmetic Reasoning: word problems. Practice translating words → equations. Rate/time/distance, percentages, ratios.
  • Word Knowledge: 20 new words/day, roots & prefixes, flashcards (spaced repetition).
  • Paragraph Comprehension: read the question first, then the passage. Practice main-idea & inference.

Priority 2 — line-score subtests

Study these after AFQT is solid, and only the ones your target MOS needs.

  • General Science (GS): HS biology/chem/physics basics
  • Electronics Info (EI): circuits, Ohm's law, terms
  • Mechanical Comprehension (MC): pulleys, levers, force
  • Auto & Shop (AS): tools, engine basics
  • Assembling Objects (AO): spatial puzzles — pattern practice

Body Track · 05

Fitness & the AFT

The Army Fitness Test (AFT) replaced the ACFT — five events, scored 0–100 each, 60 minimum per event, 300 to pass. You're moderately active, so the plan is: build a safe base first, hit passing by the mid-point, then push into competitive scores. Combat-arms MOS need a higher, sex-neutral 350 standard.

Milestones

Fitness Milestones

0/6

Injury-proofing

  • Warm up + mobility every session — non-negotiable
  • Increase run volume ≤10%/week
  • Deload every 4th week; no maxing while fatigued
  • Shin/knee niggle → back off early, don't push through

Fuel & recovery

  • Protein at every meal; carbs around training
  • Hydrate — the SDC & run punish dehydration
  • 7–8 hrs sleep — this is where you actually get fitter
  • One full rest day/week, taken seriously
Safety: This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice. Get cleared by a doctor before starting, especially before max-effort lifts or timed runs. Stop and seek help for chest pain, dizziness, or sharp joint pain.

Living Manual · 07

Refine the Plan

A plan that never changes is a plan that's wrong by Day 30. This manual is meant to evolve with your real numbers, your recruiter's guidance, and what actually works. Here's the ritual that keeps it sharp.

Phase-review ritual (every 30 days)

At the end of each phase, spend 30 minutes and answer:

  • Hit rate: which milestones did I clear? Which slipped, and why?
  • Real numbers: what do revenue, run time, and practice AFQT actually say?
  • Reality check: is the next phase's target still right — too easy, too hard, wrong entirely?
  • The decision drift: am I more or less sure about enlisting? Why? (You're "still exploring" — this question is the whole point of the 180 days.)
  • Cut & double-down: what one thing do I stop, and what one thing do I go harder on?

Then bring me the answers and we rewrite the next phase together.
